Jordan Pike: Thanks for making time today, Valerie. I'd like to understand how your team handles loss trend reporting now and where it slows you down. Does that work for you?

Valerie Strout: Sure. Right now my team pulls claims data from a few different systems and assembles the trend reports by hand. It takes most of a month after quarter close before leadership sees anything.

Jordan Pike: That's a long lag. Where does most of the time go, the data pulls or the analysis itself?

Valerie Strout: Mostly the prep. Reconciling the claims extracts, fixing coding differences, and rebuilding the same spreadsheets each cycle. The actual analysis is the smaller part, which is frustrating.

Jordan Pike: Understood. When the reports finally reach leadership, what decisions depend on them?

Valerie Strout: Reserving discussions and pricing reviews, mainly. If we spot a trend late, we're reacting instead of adjusting. Our underwriting group has asked more than once for something closer to monthly.

Jordan Pike: So the goal is a faster cycle, ideally monthly, without adding headcount to do the manual prep.

Valerie Strout: Exactly. I also need the numbers to be auditable. Our finance and compliance teams will ask how every figure was produced.

Jordan Pike: That makes sense. Have you looked at any tooling for this yet, or is this early exploration?

Valerie Strout: Early. I've talked it through internally, but nothing formal. I'd need to bring in our IT group before we get far, since the claims data sits in several places.

Jordan Pike: That's a good instinct. Here's what I'd suggest for next steps: I'll send you a short list of questions about your data sources so IT can review them, and then we can schedule a demo built around your loss trend workflow.

Valerie Strout: That works. Send the questions over and I'll get them to IT this week.

Jordan Pike: Will do. I'll follow up after the call with the questions and a couple of times for the demo.

Valerie Strout: Sounds good. Thanks, Jordan.
