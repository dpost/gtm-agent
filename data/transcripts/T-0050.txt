Jordan Pike: Thanks for making time, Bram. I know fleet planning is busy. I'd like to understand how your team works today and what you'd want from reporting.

Bram Hoekstra: Sure. Right now we build vessel schedules in spreadsheets and pull port data from a few different sources. It takes a lot of manual work to keep everything lined up.

Jordan Pike: What part of that is the most painful?

Bram Hoekstra: Schedule changes. When a vessel is delayed, we have to update several files by hand, and then the port reports are out of date until someone fixes them.

Jordan Pike: So the main need is a single view of the schedule that updates the port reporting alongside it.

Bram Hoekstra: Exactly. We'd also want to see port call performance over time, like how long vessels wait at berth in each port.

Jordan Pike: That's something we can build. Who would use it day to day?

Bram Hoekstra: The planning team mostly, and a few people in operations who need to read the reports. I'd guess a small group at first.

Jordan Pike: That helps. Do you have data in a system now, or is it mostly files?

Bram Hoekstra: A mix. Schedules are in files, and port data comes from a scheduling system that can export. Our IT group would need to look at how to connect it.

Jordan Pike: Understood. I'll bring one of our solutions engineers to the next call so we can talk through the data connection with your IT contact.
